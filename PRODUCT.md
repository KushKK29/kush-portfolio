# Product

## Register

brand

## Platform

web

## Users

Three overlapping audiences land here: recruiters and hiring managers evaluating Kush for full-time or internship roles, potential clients scouting freelance/contract work, and fellow developers discovering him through GitHub or social links. Most arrive with limited time and a skimming mindset — they're deciding in seconds whether to keep reading, and the site has to earn attention from all three without diluting into generic "something for everyone."

## Product Purpose

A personal portfolio for Kush Goel that showcases projects, skills, experience, and education, built to be the first impression a stranger gets before deciding to reach out. Success is a visitor moving from browsing to action: starting a conversation about a role, opening the resume, or exploring a case study deeply enough to trust the work behind it.

## Positioning

A portfolio that proves engineering depth through the site itself — the 3D navigation, the case studies, the craft of the build — not just through a list of claims about the person who made it.

## Conversion & proof

- Primary and secondary CTA: start a conversation about a job opportunity (contact/chatbot); fallback is downloading the resume for self-serve evaluation.
- The line a visitor remembers after 10 seconds: this person builds real, working things — and the site proves it.
- Belief ladder: this looks like serious engineering work, not a template → the projects and case studies back that up with real detail → this person is worth a conversation.
- Proof on hand: four written case studies (Articleship, GeeksforGeeks, Humantic AI, Stock Analyser) in `src/content/caseStudies/`; live resume link surfaced from the Intro section.

## Brand Personality

Confident and technical: precise, credible, engineering-forward. The personality comes from letting the work and the craft of the build speak plainly rather than from decoration — confidence shows up as restraint and functioning detail (the 3D scene, the chatbot, the case studies), not as loud visual flourish.

## Anti-references

Not a generic templated portfolio — no stock hero-plus-card-grid-of-projects layout that reads as bootcamp-issue. Not overly corporate or stiff either; it shouldn't feel like a resume PDF reformatted as a webpage.

## Design Principles

- Prove, don't claim: every section should demonstrate capability (working 3D scene, functioning chatbot, detailed case studies) rather than assert it in adjectives.
- Skimmable first, deep second: a visitor deciding in seconds needs instant legibility, with real depth available to whoever chooses to dig into a case study.
- Restraint reads as confidence: technical polish and functioning detail carry the personality, not decorative noise.
- Serve three audiences without diluting for any of them: recruiters, clients, and developers should each find what they need without the site feeling unfocused.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA baseline: keyboard-navigable UI, sufficient color contrast, and respect for `prefers-reduced-motion` — the 3D scene already falls back to the classic layout on reduced-motion and small screens; that pattern should extend to any new motion work.
